# Differentiated Existence Model (DEM)

**Differentiated Existence Model** is a developing foundational research framework that starts from **existence** and **differentiation**, then asks which familiar structures of physics must actually be fundamental and which may instead be emergent under a particular resolution.

> **Status:** early research program. DEM is not peer reviewed, experimentally confirmed, or a completed Theory of Everything.

## Thesis in one sentence

> **Start with existence. Admit differentiation. Construct everything else cautiously, and never mistake a stabilized resolution of differentiated existence for existence itself.**

DEM is deliberately conservative about claims. Established physics is treated as a hard compatibility requirement. Coordinates, partitions, measurement conventions, and useful mathematical representations are not automatically promoted into ontology. Speculative ideas are labeled, and progress must eventually produce derivations, invariant observables, or falsifiable predictions.

## What DEM is trying to avoid

A recurring failure mode in foundational reasoning is to draw a useful boundary in a model and then forget that the boundary may have come from the model. DEM calls this the **anti-smuggling rule**:

> Do not silently promote coordinate, partition, resolution, or measurement conventions into fundamental ontology.

That rule applies to DEM itself. Terms such as *dimensional fabric*, *propensity*, or *resolution* do not become explanations merely because they are general. They must eventually earn their place mathematically.

## Scientific obligation

A viable physical realization of DEM must recover the successful predictions of relativity, quantum mechanics, quantum field theory, and statistical mechanics in their tested domains. Reinterpretation is allowed; throwing away successful observables is not.

The immediate priority is therefore **formalization**, not publicity: construct the smallest model that can calculate something nontrivial, recover known physics, and expose a way to be wrong.
