# DEM Research Program

DEM is not ready for broad physical claims until its ontology becomes calculational. The immediate goal is to turn the framework into the smallest mathematical model that can succeed or fail clearly.

## Phase 1 — Formalize the substrate

1. Define the smallest primitive set and dependency graph.
2. Choose the least mathematical substrate able to represent variable dimensional participation: candidate families include differential/state manifolds, fiber bundles, graphs/networks, category/process structures, or another construction.
3. Define the resolution operator `R` precisely and state what each resolution preserves and discards.
4. Define a metric or generalized relation on resolved modes without assuming ordinary spacetime at the foundation.
5. Separate coordinate effects from invariant physical structure.

### Exit criterion

Two independent researchers should be able to calculate the same resolved state from the same formal definitions.

## Phase 2 — Recover an effective relativistic regime

1. Build the smallest toy model in which temporal ordering is derived from local state/process comparison rather than introduced as a fundamental global time axis.
2. Recover a Lorentzian effective geometry.
3. Derive an invariant causal scale and determine what deeper parameter or coupling fixes it.
4. Recover proper-time comparisons, relativity of simultaneity, and local light-cone structure.
5. Show explicitly why ordinary controllable signals remain inside the effective light cone.

### Exit criterion

The toy model reproduces the relevant special-relativistic observables exactly inside its intended regime.

## Phase 3 — Stable objecthood

1. Define a correlation/stability criterion under which a bundle of differentiated modes behaves as a persistent object.
2. Determine whether topological stabilization is necessary, sufficient, or merely one implementation.
3. Recover inertial behavior for a stable object in the effective geometric regime.
4. Determine what mass-like quantities correspond to in the underlying model.

### Candidate implementation track: vortex/coherent-field dynamics

Study Gross–Pitaevskii-like coherent-field systems and related vortex models as **candidate implementations**, not foundations. Questions include:

- Can persistent vortex knots or links realize DEM objecthood?
- What resolution-independent features correspond to topology, circulation, or phase winding?
- Can object-like persistence survive changes in effective dimensional participation?
- Can particle-like mass/spin/charge structure be derived rather than assigned?

## Phase 4 — Propensity discrimination

Before inventing a new physical quantity, test the provisional DEM quantity `P` against established structures:

- generalized momentum;
- action;
- Hamiltonian flow;
- stress-energy;
- covectors/tensors on state space;
- conserved Noether charges;
- quantum-state invariants.

Candidate projection picture:

```text
p_i = projection(P, e_i(R))
```

### Exit criterion

Either `P` demonstrably adds explanatory or predictive leverage, or the term is retired in favor of an existing mathematical object.

## Phase 5 — Fluid singularity / resolution-boundary study

OpenAI's September 2026 proposed Navier–Stokes solution provides a concrete mathematical case in which smooth continuum fluid dynamics can produce a finite-time singularity. This motivates a narrow DEM question:

> Can singular behavior in an effective continuum theory be characterized formally as the point at which discarded degrees of freedom become dynamically indispensable?

Research tasks:

1. Reproduce the qualitative mathematical structure of vortex focusing in a simpler toy system.
2. Compare classical continuum evolution with a microscopic or coherent-field model that has additional short-scale degrees of freedom.
3. Determine whether the latter regularizes the singular limit and identify the invariant condition at which the effective description fails.
4. Avoid interpreting regularization as evidence for a specific vacuum model unless independent physics supports it.

## Phase 6 — Quantum compatibility

Only after the earlier structure is mathematically stable:

1. Construct an entanglement toy model in which separated resolved endpoints may be projections of deeper common structure.
2. Recover quantum probabilities rather than merely qualitative correlation.
3. Reproduce Bell-inequality violations without falling back to Bell-local hidden variables.
4. Recover standard no-signalling marginals unless DEM explicitly predicts otherwise.
5. Distinguish common generative structure from controllable communication.

## Phase 7 — One decisive discriminant

Prefer one narrow prediction over many loose reinterpretations.

High-value target classes include:

- a coordinate-invariant dimensional-stretch observable;
- a quantitatively derived correction to a known effective relation;
- a resolution-transition signature in a system with independently known microscopic dynamics;
- a demonstrable new relation among otherwise independent observables.

A controllable spacelike communication channel would be decisive, but DEM must not mistake Bell correlation, pre-shared structure, leakage, or synchronization error for such a channel.

## Failure criteria

A candidate DEM formalization should be rejected or substantially revised if it:

- fails established precision tests in its claimed regime;
- produces only coordinate artifacts;
- cannot recover relativistic clock structure;
- reduces entanglement to excluded Bell-local variables;
- introduces new terminology without mathematical leverage;
- protects every claim from falsification by changing interpretation after the fact.

## Near-term deliverable

The next concrete artifact should be a **minimal formal toy model**, not another broad conceptual essay. It should define primitives, state space, resolution, metric structure, dynamics, observables, and one explicit recovery target from established physics.
